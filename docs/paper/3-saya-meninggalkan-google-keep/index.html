I Stopped Using Google Keep For Daily Task.

18 September 2026

Yowza, folks!

As of September 10, 2026, your boy from the admin team is officially dumping Google Keep for task tracking. Everything’s moving over to https://sukalaper-google-keep-lite.vercel.app/, a little tool I whipped up myself with Supabase and Spreadsheets running under the hood.

Might feel like reinventing the wheel since there are tons of apps out there doing the same drill, but honestly, I just wanted something tailor-made for my daily workflow and, more importantly, to dodge that annoying vendor lock-in.

To break it down, here are the two big pain points that made me jump ship:

  1. Hitting the Brutal 1,000-Checklist Cap: Once you hit Keep’s 1,000-item ceiling, you’re toast. Yeah, sure, I could just trash finished tasks to clear up space, but no way. I still need that backlog to double-check old work or look back at project milestones. Nuking my history just to bypass a dumb system limit ain’t it.

  2. Zero Automatic Created Timestamps: I want my stuff clean and structured. I need to know the exact moment a task dropped without typing out dates like a caveman. Keep forces you to log that manually, whereas my new tool handles it out of the box.

My game plan is stupidly simple: punch in a task, let the timestamp pop up automatically, slap a label on it, edit or delete when needed, and call it a day. No clutter, no nonsense.

Don’t get it twisted though: I ain’t throwing shade or calling Keep total trash. The thing’s been a lifesaver and held down the fort for me big time. But with the hard limits and pain points I just broke down, we’re just not vibing on the same wavelength anymore. If Keep still hits the sweet spot for your own daily grind, by all means, you do you and rock on! As for me? Time to bounce.

Cheers!
